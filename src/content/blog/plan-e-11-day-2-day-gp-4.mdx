## 01 — The shape of the trip

Compact, cost-efficient, and structured around two Monaco days that are deliberately different in character. Sunday is a hospitality day at Casino Square — table-seated, paced, paid-for. Monday is a roaming day — Prince's Car Collection in the morning, walking the just-dismantled circuit, supercars cruising Casino Square as wealthy GP guests linger before flying home.

**What you get:** Sunday Race at F1 Experiences Amazonico Terrace (~₹2.47L family) + Monday public Monaco day (no hospitality cost, ~₹15K transport + Car Collection + lunch). Two distinct Monaco experiences — one hospitality, one wandering — at a fraction of the cost of a Saturday Qualifying day.

**What you trade vs the 13-day plans:** Saturday Qualifying — widely considered the most thrilling F1 session at Monaco — is on the room TV, not in person. You also lose one Versailles night (1 instead of 2) and one Provence night (2 instead of 3). Tuesday buffer day before flying home is also gone — you fly home Tuesday afternoon instead.

**What this plan optimizes for:** The two Monaco days Neil cares about most (the Race + the supercars), the Provençal countryside experience, and a manageable cost (~₹6L cheaper than the 13-day three-Monaco-day plan). Recovery infrastructure is single-night Versailles + Auberge de Cassagne's spa + 5 protected Riviera days before the GP.

### Châteauneuf-du-Pape — Three estates worth knowing

- **Château de Beaucastel** — among the most prestigious globally, biodynamic since the 1950s. Harder to book, more formal.

All need **advance booking** (3–4 weeks minimum). Auberge de Cassagne's concierge can arrange. Per-person tasting fee: ~€25–60.

## 05 — Three critical mitigations

Two consecutive Monaco days demand careful pacing. If any of these slip, the GP weekend turns from once-in-a-lifetime into fibromyalgia flare. Treat them as non-negotiable.

### 01 · Friday + Saturday rest stack

Two protected rest days before Sunday's race day. Friday 5 June and Saturday 6 June are both full hotel-pool-and-balcony rest days. No outings. No "quick lunch in Èze." No "we feel fine, let's pop into Old Nice." The single biggest determinant of whether Sunday's race day is bearable is how rested you are when Sunday morning arrives. The fact that Saturday's GP qualifying is happening 50 km away is irrelevant — watch it on the room TV. Treat both as scheduled medical recovery.

### 02 · Sunday post-race private taxi

Pre-book a private car for the post-race departure. €200 for the family is the single best fibromyalgia investment in this entire trip. The Monaco train station post-race on Sunday evening is the worst physical environment of the 11 days — 50,000+ people queuing for trains, multiple platforms, standing-only trains, 30–45 minute waits. A pre-booked taxi via Hyatt concierge (book 4 weeks before) gets you door-to-door in 35 minutes vs 90+ minutes by train.

### 03 · Tuesday departure timing

No Tuesday buffer day in this 11-day plan. Monday Monaco is followed directly by Tuesday's flight home. Mitigations: Monday 21:30 last-train-back (don't push it to last-train-out-of-Monaco), Tuesday flight at 14:00 or later (not early-morning), light room-service dinner Monday, slow Tuesday morning at Hyatt.

If your wife's fatigue is high after Monday Monaco, Tuesday morning becomes critical. Build in a slow checkout (12:00 if hotel allows), a late lunch on the way to NCE airport, and don't try to do anything Tuesday morning except packing and resting.

### Bonus · Foam earplugs from India

Don't trust on-site sellers — they price-gouge during GP weekend. F1 cars at the Monaco tunnel exit hit ~110 dB. Bring proper foam earplugs (Loop Engage, Eargasm, or Mack's foam) and child-rated ear defenders for Neil. Three sets per person — Sunday venue + Monday circuit walk where teams may still be running engines.

### Paris dipping rules — if you have time on Monday morning

> **Five rules to skip the tourist traps** (probably not relevant for this 1-night Versailles stay, but kept here for reference):
>
> 1. **Don't eat within 300m** of the Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Notre-Dame, Champs-Élysées, or Sacré-Cœur.
> 2. **Skip restaurants with laminated photo menus** or staff trying to wave you in.
> 3. **For lunch, head to Le Marais** (3rd/4th arr.) — vegetarian-friendly bistros, 15–20 min Métro from the Eiffel Tower area.
> 4. **Skip the Tuileries tuk-tuk rides.** Skip **Angelina** (long lines, overrated). Skip the Mona Lisa scrum at the Louvre.
> 5. **Use [Le Fooding](https://lefooding.com)** for credible restaurant picks. Skip TripAdvisor and TikTok recommendations.

## 11 — Optional add-ons & alternatives

![Breathtaking coastal view of the French Riviera with rooftops](https://images.pexels.com/photos/30714165/pexels-photo-30714165.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

The plan above is the recommended path. Each of these is a flexible swap or add-on — pick what fits energy levels and interest on the day. Friday rest, Sunday race day, and the flight legs are non-negotiable; everything else has options.

### Wed 3 Jun · Châteauneuf-du-Pape — pick your estate

The default is Château La Nerthe. Two alternatives if you want a different vibe:

| Estate | Style | Best for | Booking lead time |

### Thu 4 Jun · Riviera exploration — three flavours

The default is Old Nice + Castle Hill morning, hotel rest afternoon. Two alternatives if you want a different Thursday shape:

#### Option A · Old Nice morning + Cap Ferrat afternoon *(if energy is high)*

Add a half-day to Cap Ferrat after the morning Old Nice + Castle Hill. Cost: ~€150 driver round-trip from Hyatt.

#### Option B · Saint-Paul-de-Vence inland day *(swap, not add)*

#### Option D · Old Nice morning + Èze Jardin Exotique afternoon *(panoramic-views version)*

![Aerial view of Èze village with the Mediterranean Sea](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7997666/pexels-photo-7997666.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

Add a 90-minute Èze gardens visit after the morning Old Nice + Castle Hill. Cost: ~€120 driver round-trip from Hyatt.

The full Èze village experience is **fibromyalgia-hostile** — cobblestones, steep paths, narrow stairs everywhere. **Skip the village itself.** But the **Jardin Exotique** at the very top is a different proposition:

- Bus / car accessible (driver drops at the entrance)
- Sea-cliff cactus garden ~429m above the Mediterranean
- The most panoramic view on the entire Riviera — Cap Ferrat, the bay, sometimes Corsica on a clear day
- ~30 min walking on uneven but mostly flat paths inside the gardens
- Benches throughout, easy to pace
- 12th-century chapel ruins at the top for the photo

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| 09:00 | Old Nice + Castle Hill via elevator (as default morning) |
| 13:00 | Light lunch at Hyatt or Promenade café |
| 14:30 | Driver to Èze (~25 min) — drop at the Jardin Exotique entrance, NOT the village. |
| 15:00 | **Jardin Exotique** — slow walk through cactus terraces, sea-cliff viewpoints, 12th-century chapel ruins. ~60–90 min. Sit at any bench whenever needed. |
| 16:30 | Driver back to Hyatt. |
| 17:30 | Pool / spa / balcony rest. |
| 19:30 | Light dinner at Hyatt. |

**Pro:** The single most photographed view on the Riviera. Genuinely unique — the cactus-meets-Mediterranean look exists nowhere else nearby. Easier than the village.
**Con:** Still a 4-hour outing on top of the morning. Adds fatigue before the GP weekend. Skip if energy is borderline.

**Important:** If you go to Èze, **stay at the Jardin Exotique only**. Do NOT try to "just have a quick look at the village" — the village walk is steep cobblestones with stairs, and there's no easy "elevator" route like Castle Hill in Nice. The driver brief should be explicit: "Jardin Exotique entrance only, do not stop in the village."

### Other options we deliberately rejected

| Place | Why we skipped |

| **Saint-Tropez** | 1h45 each way from Nice. Wrong audience (nightlife/celebrity-yacht scene). Antibes Port Vauban gives 80% of the experience at 25 min. |

| **Arles & Nîmes (Roman ruins)** | Same — already familiar with Roman architecture from Turkey. |

### Quick decision rules

When in doubt:

1. **If energy is borderline — choose the lower-effort option.** Fibromyalgia recovery isn't linear; one over-extension can flatten three days.
2. **If your wife wants to skip an outing — skip it without negotiation.** Her energy is the binding constraint, not yours or Neil's.
3. **If a Friday/Saturday energy-banking day starts feeling fine by 14:00 — STILL DON'T ADD ANYTHING MAJOR.** The whole point of these days is to over-rest before the GP. Boredom is the goal, not a problem.
4. **If Sunday's race day depletes you more than expected — collapse Monday's plan.** Half-day Monaco is fine. Full hotel-pool-Monday is also fine. The Prince's Car Collection will be there in 2027 if needed.
