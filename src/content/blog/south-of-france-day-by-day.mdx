![Lavender and stone — the South of France in early summer](https://images.pexels.com/photos/32992520/pexels-photo-32992520.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

Two weeks out, the plan is locked. Flights paid, hotels booked, drivers confirmed, Grand Prix grandstand tickets in hand. What started as a "we should see the Monaco GP one day" idea now has a day-by-day shape — Lufthansa Business south through Frankfurt, a scenic road from the Côte d'Azur through Aix-en-Provence to a Relais & Châteaux country house outside Avignon, five nights of vineyards and hilltop villages, a direct TGV back to the Riviera, three slow Mediterranean days, and the Monaco Grand Prix as the finale.

This post is the actual operational plan — twelve days, eleven nights, two countries (France and a Monégasque afternoon), one bucket-list race. With route maps for each leg, so you can see exactly how the trip stitches together.

## The shape of the trip

The trip is two halves connected by a single train ride:

- **The Provençal half** — five nights at [Auberge de Provence](https://maps.google.com/maps?q=Auberge+de+Provence+Villeneuve-l%C3%A8s-Avignon&output=embed), a country auberge near Avignon, with day trips to the Luberon villages, Châteauneuf-du-Pape vineyards, and the Alpilles cultural triangle of Saint-Rémy / Carrières / Les Baux.
- **The Riviera half** — five nights at [Le Méridien Nice](https://maps.google.com/maps?q=Le+Meridien+Nice&output=embed) on the Promenade des Anglais, with a Michelin lunch in Èze, the Monaco Grand Prix on Sunday, and a post-race wind-down on Monday.

A direct TGV connects them — three and a half hours, scenic Mediterranean coastline most of the way.

## Day 01 · Fri 29 May — Mumbai → Frankfurt → Nice

**Route:** BOM (02:40) → FRA (Lufthansa LH757) → NCE (LH1060, arrive 13:45). Overnight at [Novotel Suites Nice Airport](https://maps.google.com/maps?q=Novotel+Suites+Nice+Airport&output=embed).

A pre-dawn departure from Mumbai. Lufthansa Business through Frankfurt, lie-flat from BOM to FRA, a tight but protected single-PNR connection at FRA, and into Nice mid-afternoon. The Nice airport sits on a man-made coastal platform with the sea on one side and the city skyline on the other — landing is one of the more dramatic descents in European aviation.

Rather than rushing into the city or onto a TGV the same day, we've booked a single utility night at the Novotel Suites Nice Airport — a 5-minute taxi from the terminal. The point is simple: arrive, eat something light, sleep. The real trip starts on Saturday morning, rested.

> **Why an airport hotel night?** The temptation after a 10-hour transit is to push for Avignon the same day. We've done that math. Two more transfers and a TGV after a Lufthansa long-haul is the recipe for arriving at the Auberge wrecked. The Novotel night costs roughly €240 and is the best €240 of the trip.

## Day 02 · Sat 30 May — The scenic road from the coast to Provence

**Route:** Nice airport → Aix-en-Provence (lunch) → [Auberge de Provence](https://maps.google.com/maps?q=Auberge+de+Provence+Villeneuve-l%C3%A8s-Avignon&output=embed), Villeneuve-lès-Avignon. Distance ~265 km, scenic via A8/A7 with the Aix detour. Driver: Gordana (private guide, Provence with Gordana).

The plan was originally a TGV south — Nice-Ville to Avignon, fast and efficient. But the GP-week TGV schedule into Avignon is thin on direct trains, and a TGV doesn't let you see anything. So we pivoted to a **private scenic transfer with a licensed Provence guide** — Gordana, who came to us via the Auberge with 20 years of consistently excellent reviews.

The road from Nice takes the autoroute past Cannes, swings inland near Aubagne, and arrives at **Aix-en-Provence** in time for lunch. Cézanne's home town: a wide Cours Mirabeau lined with double rows of plane trees, baroque fountains, cafés that have been around since 1792 (*Les Deux Garçons*, where Cézanne and Zola used to argue). A two-hour lunch in Aix, a slow stroll along the Cours, then back on the road for the remaining 90 minutes into Villeneuve-lès-Avignon, the village across the Rhône from Avignon proper.

![Provençal countryside — vineyards, plane trees, golden afternoon light](https://images.pexels.com/photos/33486886/pexels-photo-33486886.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

We arrive at Auberge de Provence by late afternoon — pool, gardens, vineyard views, and the first of five nights at the Provençal base. A light Provençal dinner at the hotel restaurant, then sleep.

## Day 03 · Sun 31 May — Provence settles in

**Route:** Auberge de Provence (no travel). Pool, gardens, hotel restaurant.

The first full Provençal day is deliberately empty. Five nights at the Auberge gives us the luxury of one day where nothing is scheduled — no driver, no excursion, no town to get to. We use it for the spa, the pool, the gardens, a slow breakfast on the terrace under the plane trees, and a long Provençal lunch at the hotel restaurant.

The pace of the entire trip turns on this day. Provence isn't somewhere you "do" — it's somewhere you slow down inside. The point of arriving on Saturday and not scheduling anything for Sunday is to actually feel that shift.

![Mediterranean afternoon — the Provence rhythm](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7975171/pexels-photo-7975171.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

If energy is good, Avignon old town is a 15-minute taxi away — a brief late-afternoon stroll past the Palais des Papes (the 14th-century papal fortress where seven popes lived after the move from Rome) and along the Rhône with views of the famous half-bridge (*Pont Saint-Bénézet*, the one in the children's song). But it's optional, not booked, and the default is the Auberge.

## Day 04 · Mon 1 Jun — Luberon villages

**Route:** Auberge → Gordes → Sénanque Abbey → Roussillon → Auberge. Group tour with Provence with Gordana, 5 hours, pickup from Avignon tourist office. Distance ~110 km round-trip.

A half-day group tour through the Luberon — the hilltop-village heartland of inland Provence. Three stops, all classified among *Les Plus Beaux Villages de France* (the Most Beautiful Villages of France):

- **Gordes**, the village from every Provence postcard — stone houses stacked into a hillside above a wide olive-and-vine valley, with the Luberon range rising on the horizon. A short walk, a viewpoint photo, time for coffee on the main square.
- **Sénanque Abbey**, a 12th-century Cistercian monastery in a quiet valley below Gordes. The lavender fields in front of the abbey are the ones that get photographed in every Provence brochure — peak bloom is late June through mid-July, so we're three weeks early and they'll be green, not purple. The abbey itself is the point: a Romanesque cloister, simple lines, working monks still in residence.
- **Roussillon**, the ochre village — built on what was once the largest ochre deposit in France, the entire village is painted in tones of red, orange, and yellow that come straight from the cliffs below. The contrast against blue summer sky is the point.

![Gordes — the iconic hilltop village of the Luberon](https://images.pexels.com/photos/31992435/pexels-photo-31992435.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

The tour returns to Avignon by 14:00. We taxi back to the Auberge for a late lunch and the afternoon off.

## Day 05 · Tue 2 Jun — Châteauneuf-du-Pape with a Master of Wine

**Route:** Auberge → Châteauneuf-du-Pape → village cellar → 3 estate visits → Auberge. Full day with Romain Méric of *Provence and Wine*, 09:30–17:30. Distance ~50 km.

The wine day. Châteauneuf-du-Pape is 25 minutes north of the Auberge — a small village built around the ruins of a 14th-century papal summer château, surrounded by some of the most prestigious vineyards in the southern Rhône. The appellation's signature is rare in the wine world: 13 permitted grape varieties (most appellations allow one or two), grown on the famous *galets roulés* — large smooth stones that store the day's heat and release it back to the vines at night.

We're going with **Romain Méric**, who holds the DipWSET certification (the highest non-MW wine credential, with under 12,000 holders globally) and has run *Provence and Wine* for 20 years. The day is structured as:

- **Morning**: village wine cellar for an overview tasting and orientation on the appellation's history, geology, and grape varieties.
- **Late morning to mid-afternoon**: four estate visits — including a prestige producer (e.g., Château La Nerthe or Domaine du Vieux Télégraphe) and a smaller family estate. Cellar tour and tasting at each.
- **Lunch**: a vineyard-estate restaurant Romain selects (vegetarian-friendly, confirmed in advance).
- **Late afternoon**: a brief stop at the village château ruins at the summit, with views across the appellation toward Mont Ventoux.

![Vineyards in early summer — the southern Rhône](https://images.pexels.com/photos/14208800/pexels-photo-14208800.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

We're back at the Auberge by early evening. Light dinner at the hotel.

## Day 06 · Wed 3 Jun — Saint-Rémy, Carrières de Lumières, Les Baux

**Route:** Auberge → Saint-Rémy-de-Provence → Carrières de Lumières → Les Baux-de-Provence → Auberge. Full day with Provence with Gordana (private), 09:30–17:30. Distance ~80 km round-trip.

The cultural day. Three stops, all in the Alpilles — a small limestone mountain range south of Avignon — and structured to be light on the feet:

- **Saint-Rémy-de-Provence** (09:30–11:30) — the town where Van Gogh checked himself into the Saint-Paul-de-Mausole asylum in 1889 and painted 150 canvases in the surrounding year, including *The Starry Night*. We walk Place de la République, see Nostradamus's birthplace, drive past the Roman *Mausoleum of the Antiques* and *Arc de Triomphe* on the way out of town. No climbing.
- **Lunch in Saint-Rémy** — a café or bistro on Place Jean Jaurès.
- **Carrières de Lumières** (14:00–15:30) — the most distinctive experience of the Provençal stretch. A former limestone quarry below Les Baux, converted into an immersive art installation: monumental projected art (Klimt, Van Gogh, Cézanne in different years) moves across 14-metre quarry walls while you sit on benches in the cool dark. The 2026 program is yet to be confirmed, but the format has been consistently extraordinary since the venue opened in 2012.
- **Les Baux-de-Provence** (15:30–17:00) — a medieval village built into a rocky spur with a ruined fortress at the summit. We see it from below for the photo, walk the lower village briefly, and skip the citadel climb (steep, no shade, not the point of the day).

![Provence in late afternoon light — the kind of texture Van Gogh painted](https://images.pexels.com/photos/33486886/pexels-photo-33486886.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1600)

Back at the Auberge by 17:30 for the final Provençal dinner.

## Day 07 · Thu 4 Jun — TGV back to the Riviera

**Route:** Auberge → Avignon TGV → Nice-Ville. Direct TGV, ~3h 36min. Then short transfer to [Le Méridien Nice](https://maps.google.com/maps?q=Le+Meridien+Nice&output=embed).

The pivot day. Check out of the Auberge after a slow breakfast, taxi 10 minutes to Avignon TGV station, board a direct Avignon–Nice train (one of three direct services per day — most go via Marseille with a change), and watch the Provençal landscape give way to the Mediterranean coast over three and a half hours. The final 90 minutes hug the coast — Cassis, La Ciotat, Toulon, Saint-Raphaël, Cannes — with sea views from the right-side window almost the entire way.

We arrive at Nice-Ville mid-afternoon and taxi 8 minutes to Le Méridien on the Promenade des Anglais. Check in, unpack, and walk five minutes east into Old Nice for a first orientation — the ochre alleys of the *Vieille Ville*, a gelato at *Fenocchio* (the city's institutional gelateria, 95 flavours), and a slow drink on Place Rossetti.

## Day 08 · Fri 5 Jun — Old Nice and the Mediterranean

**Route:** Le Méridien — Old Nice walking. Optional: Villefranche-sur-Mer (7 min by train) or sunset cruise.

A slow day, anchored in Nice itself. The Côte d'Azur was named that — Coast of Azure — for a reason, and the best way to feel it is to sit on the Promenade des Anglais in the morning sun with a coffee and watch the Mediterranean do its blue-to-turquoise gradient.

The Nice plan for the day:

- **Cours Saleya** — the open-air flower and produce market in Old Nice, every morning except Monday. Pyramids of tomatoes, lavender bunches, olive oils, soaps, and the queue at *Lou Pilha Leva* for socca (the chickpea-flour pancake from a wood-fired oven that's the city's signature street food).
- **Castle Hill** (*Colline du Château*) — a 90-metre hilltop park between Old Nice and the port, with the best panoramic view of the bay. There's an elevator next to the *I Love Nice* sign on Quai des États-Unis that bypasses the 213 stairs to the top.
- **Late afternoon** — the optional outing. Either a **sunset cruise** from the port of Nice (~€60pp, 90 minutes along the coast toward Cap Ferrat at golden hour), or a **train to Villefranche-sur-Mer** (7 minutes, €4) for the pastel-painted port and a sunset dinner at a harbour-side terrace.

The Friday plan is deliberately picked-from-a-menu — we'll decide morning-of based on energy and weather.

## Day 09 · Sat 6 Jun — Èze and the eve of the GP

**Route:** Le Méridien → Èze village → Le Méridien → pre-GP dinner. Private driver round-trip, ~30 min each way.

The marquee day-out before race day. **Èze** is the medieval village perched 429 metres above the Mediterranean — the most photographed view on the entire Côte d'Azur. The lower coastal road from Nice to Monaco passes underneath; Èze itself is reached by a steep switchback road up the cliff.

We've booked a private driver round-trip (~€120–180) because the village is steep cobblestone and we want the option to leave when energy says so. The plan:

- **11:00** — driver pickup at Le Méridien.
- **11:30** — arrive Èze village. A slow stroll through the medieval lanes (steep but short), with the famous viewpoint over the bay toward Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat.
- **13:00–15:00** — long lunch at **Château Eza** (one Michelin star), on a cliffside terrace with the same sea view from your plate. Mediterranean menu, Provençal accent, the wine list goes deep on regional whites and rosés.
- **15:30** — driver pickup, back to Le Méridien by 16:00.
- **Late afternoon** — pool, rest, watch the GP qualifying session on TV.
- **20:00** — pre-GP dinner in Nice (booking TBD: *La Petite Maison*, *Le Plongeoir*, or *Le Chantecler*).

## Day 10 · Sun 7 Jun — Monaco Grand Prix · Race Day

**Route:** Le Méridien → Nice-Ville → Monaco-Monte Carlo (train, 25 min) → Grandstand T2 → Monaco-Monte Carlo → Nice-Ville → Le Méridien.

Race day. The big one. The 2026 Formula 1 Monaco Grand Prix, watched from **Grandstand T2** — the covered grandstand on the start-finish straight overlooking Sainte-Dévote corner, the first proper turn of the lap.

The transport plan is, perhaps surprisingly, **the train both ways**. Sounds counter-intuitive for a Grand Prix Sunday — surely the move is a private driver? — but Monaco-Monte Carlo station is a 12-minute walk from Grandstand T2, and the GP-Sunday traffic in and out of Monaco is famously brutal. The train (~€15 per family round-trip) is faster, more reliable, and avoids the road closures that fence Monaco off from the wider Riviera all weekend.

- **08:30** — wake, breakfast at Le Méridien.
- **10:30** — walk 15 minutes to Nice-Ville station (or €10 taxi).
- **11:00** — train to Monaco-Monte Carlo (25 min, direct).
- **11:30** — walk to Grandstand T2 (12 min), seat by **12:00**.
- **Picnic lunch in the grandstand** — packed from the hotel (Grandstand T2 is covered but seats are basic; bring your own water and food).
- **13:30** — F2 sprint race or support events.
- **15:00** — F1 Grand Prix start. 78 laps, approximately 2 hours.
- **17:30** — race ends. Decision: **leave on lap 65–70** (~16:30) to beat the post-race crowd, or **stay for the podium ceremony** and walk to a Monaco-side dinner before catching the **20:00 train** back.
- **Late evening** — back at Le Méridien, light dinner in the room, sleep.

## Day 11 · Mon 8 Jun — The slow lap

**Route:** Le Méridien — open day. Optional: train back to Monaco for Casino Square and the Prince's Car Collection. ~25 min each way by train.

The morning after. Casino Square reopens to pedestrians, the barriers come down across the F1 circuit, and the Prince's Car Collection at Terrasses de Fontvieille is back in business. The Monday plan is genuinely optional — depends entirely on how Sunday landed.

**If energy is good**, the morning train back to Monaco for:

- **Walking the actual F1 track** while it's being dismantled — past the swimming-pool chicane, through the tunnel exit, around Casino Square itself with the workers taking down barriers around you. The track is open to walkers from Monday morning.
- **The Prince's Car Collection** (€10 entry, on Terrasses de Fontvieille) — ~100 cars across a century of automotive history. Bugatti Type 35s, Sébastien Loeb's WRC Citroën, Charles Leclerc's Ferrari SF90, Niki Lauda's Ferrari 312T2 from the 1976 season. Two hours, mostly flat, well worth the trip.
- **Lunch in Monaco** — somewhere unremarkable. Monaco's restaurants aren't the point.
- **Afternoon train back to Nice.**

**If energy is fading** (likely after a race-day Sunday), the alternative is a slow morning at Le Méridien, a long lunch at the hotel restaurant, and an afternoon walk along the Promenade des Anglais. Same trip, much quieter pace.

## Day 12 · Tue 9 Jun — Nice → Frankfurt → Mumbai

**Route:** Le Méridien → NCE (08:45 LH2271) → FRA → BOM (LH766, arrive 23:50).

Departure morning. We've taken an early flight back to Frankfurt and a same-day connection to Mumbai — single PNR, single check-in. Late checkout at Le Méridien isn't an option on this departure (the LH flight leaves at 08:45), so the morning is brisk: wake at 06:00, taxi to NCE at 07:00, Lufthansa Senator Lounge by 07:30, board at 08:15.

Frankfurt connection is comfortable on the schedule — we land FRA at 10:25 and the BOM flight departs at 13:15, leaving about three hours including the famous T1-to-T2 walk at Frankfurt. The long-haul to Mumbai goes overnight, arriving Mumbai 23:50 local time the same day.

Twelve days. Two halves of the South of France. The Monaco Grand Prix in the middle of it.

## What we're skipping

Some honest editing. The South of France could absorb three weeks easily. We have twelve days. Things we're not doing:

- **Saint-Tropez.** An hour and forty-five minutes from Nice each way. Famous for nightlife and celebrity yachts — wrong audience for us.
- **Cannes.** The 2026 Cannes Film Festival ends 23 May, before we arrive. Without the festival, Cannes is an upmarket Promenade des Anglais. We have one of those already.
- **Saturday qualifying at Monaco.** Widely considered more thrilling than the race, but adds a third 14-hour Monaco day to a sequence that already has two. We'll watch it on the room TV.
- **Pont du Gard, Arles, Nîmes** — the Roman heritage trio. We've seen Ephesus and the full Turkish Roman cycle. The Provençal Roman sites would feel redundant.
- **Lavender fields in bloom.** Peak is late June through mid-July. We're three weeks early. The fields will be green, not purple. Sénanque Abbey we'll see — the abbey itself is the point.
- **The Gorges du Verdon and the Calanques near Cassis.** Both stunning, both demanding coastal-or-canyon hiking. Not the trip's pace.
- **Cap Ferrat, Antibes, Saint-Paul-de-Vence.** All accessible from Nice by train or short drive — kept as optional Friday picks if energy and weather cooperate.

The point of an editing list is to free up the trip for what matters most. Two halves of the South of France, one Grand Prix Sunday, eleven nights, no rushing.

## Closing

Twelve days isn't enough to "do" the South of France — but it's enough to feel both halves of it properly. The Provençal half is the authentic France of vineyards, hilltop villages, Saturday markets, and long lunches under plane trees. The Riviera half is theatre — coastline, Belle Époque hotels, the panoramic view from Èze, and the Monaco circuit at full song.

We'll write again after we're back, with real photos and real stories. For now, this is the plan.
