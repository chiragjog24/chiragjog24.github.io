## 01 — The shape of the trip

Three rest periods bracket two intensity peaks. Versailles (recovery) → Provence (recovery + exploration) → Nice (building energy) → GP weekend (peak) → buffer day → home. The Provence detour at the front is what makes the Riviera + 3 Monaco days bearable at the back.

**Why three Monaco days, not two:** Saturday is Free Practice 3 + Qualifying — widely regarded as the most thrilling session at Monaco, where the narrow track makes Sunday overtaking nearly impossible. Sunday is the race itself. Monday is the post-GP magic — Casino Square reopens, the Prince's Car Collection just unsealed for the year, supercars cruising the circuit as wealthy GP guests linger before flying home. Three different Monaco angles, not three identical days.

**Why Provence at the front, not the back:** After three consecutive 12-hour Monaco days you want to fly home to recover, not sprint through Provençal villages. Front-loading Provence puts the country-house spa stay where the fatigue is least, and uses Versailles + Provence together as a 5-day decompression runway before the Riviera.

### Châteauneuf-du-Pape — Three estates worth knowing

- **Château La Nerthe** — 18th-century estate, one of the oldest in Châteauneuf, beautiful park and historic cellars. Most "tour-ready" of the prestige estates. English-speaking guides. Vegetarian lunch sometimes available. *Recommended pick.*
- **Domaine du Vieux Télégraphe** — Brunier family, biodynamic, less touristy, more authentic working-domain feel. Great if you prefer agriculture-and-soil over château grandeur.
- **Château de Beaucastel** — among the most prestigious globally, biodynamic since the 1950s. Harder to book, more formal. For serious wine collectors.

All three need **advance booking** (3–4 weeks minimum, longer for prestige estates). Auberge de Cassagne's concierge can arrange. Per-person tasting fee: ~€25–60 depending on estate and wine flight.

### Skip-list — what NOT to add

- **Don't add a second estate visit on Wednesday.** One estate + the village is plenty; two becomes a wine slog with a TGV to catch.
- **Don't add Aix-en-Provence.** Worth its own trip; one Provence base is enough.
- **Don't try to do Avignon proper extensively.** Old town is steep cobblestones; Palais des Papes interior renovation status worth verifying for 2026.
- **Don't book a rental car.** A driver costs ~€200–€350 per session and means you can taste freely — driving plus tasting is a non-starter.
- **Don't try to see lavender fields in bloom.** Peak bloom is late June through mid-July — too early for these dates.

## 05 — Three critical mitigations

Three consecutive 12-hour Monaco days are the most demanding stretch of the trip. If any of these slip, the GP weekend turns from once-in-a-lifetime into fibromyalgia flare. Treat them as non-negotiable.

### 01 · Friday 5 June — Full rest

The day before is the day that matters. Friday is a full hotel-pool-and-balcony rest day. No outings. No "quick lunch in Èze." No "we feel fine, let's pop into Old Nice." The single biggest determinant of whether Saturday, Sunday, AND Monday are bearable is how rested you are when Saturday morning arrives. Treat it as scheduled medical recovery.

### 02 · Tuesday 9 June — Buffer recovery

After three consecutive Monaco days, Tuesday is mandatory recovery. Hotel pool, spa, balcony. No tourism. No "let's add Èze since we have time." The flight home Wednesday is hard enough; Tuesday must be a rest day, not a "soft activity" day.

### 03 · Sunday post-race private taxi

Pre-book a private car for the post-race departure. €200 for the family is the single best fibromyalgia investment in this entire trip. The Monaco train station post-race on Sunday evening is the worst physical environment of the 13 days — 50,000+ people queuing for trains, multiple platforms, standing-only trains, 30–45 minute waits. A pre-booked taxi via Hyatt concierge (book 4 weeks before) gets you door-to-door in 35 minutes vs 90+ minutes by train. Saturday and Monday departures are normal-Monaco-busy and manageable by train. Sunday is the exception.

### Bonus · Foam earplugs from India

Don't trust on-site sellers — they price-gouge during GP weekend. F1 cars at the Monaco tunnel exit hit ~110 dB. Bring proper foam earplugs (Loop Engage, Eargasm, or Mack's foam) and child-rated ear defenders for Neil. Three sets per person — two GP venue days plus a Monday circuit-walk where teams are still running engines.

### Paris dipping rules — if you venture into central Paris on Day 3

> **Five rules to skip the tourist traps:**
>
> 1. **Don't eat within 300m** of the Eiffel Tower, Louvre, Notre-Dame, Champs-Élysées, or Sacré-Cœur. Guaranteed tourist-trap zone.
> 2. **Skip restaurants with laminated photo menus** or staff trying to wave you in. Universal fast-food-tourist signal.
> 3. **For lunch, head to Le Marais** (3rd/4th arr.) instead — vegetarian-friendly bistros, walkable, 15–20 min Métro from the Eiffel Tower area. Latin Quarter (away from Rue de la Huchette) and Canal Saint-Martin are also good.
> 4. **Skip the Tuileries tuk-tuk rides.** Skip **Angelina** (the famous hot chocolate place — long lines, overrated). Skip the Mona Lisa scrum at the Louvre if you visit.
> 5. **Use [Le Fooding](https://lefooding.com)** for credible restaurant picks. Skip TripAdvisor and TikTok recommendations — both heavily skewed by tourists, not locals.
