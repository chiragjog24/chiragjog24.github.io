A practical eating guide for a France trip — Nice, Avignon, Provence, and the Monaco Grand Prix. Built around a strict vegetarian diet (no meat, no fish, no eggs) and gut + fibromyalgia sensitivities. The goal isn't to eat fearfully — France has some of the best food in the world — it's to enjoy it without stacking triggers.

## Before you go

- **Pack:** Econorm strips, NOW Peppermint oil gels, magnesium, L-theanine, chamomile teabags.
- **Timing:** Maintain supplement timing even with the timezone change.
- **Hydration:** Hydrate extra — flying + heat + fibro all add up to a real dehydration risk.

## Eat freely

### Breads & bakery

- **Baguette (white)** — refined wheat, no eggs, French staple. Your #1 breakfast bread.
- **Focaccia** — olive oil based, no eggs, safe.
- **Fougasse (Provençal bread)** — olive oil bread, typically egg-free. Safe.
- **Galettes (buckwheat crêpes)** — egg-free batter, savoury. Ask for cheese + tomato + vegetables. Your go-to crêpe option.

> Croissants, pain au chocolat, and most French pastries contain eggs (in the dough and/or as a glaze). Avoid all of these. Your safe breads are: baguette, focaccia, fougasse, and galettes. A fresh baguette with butter and jam is a perfectly satisfying French breakfast.

### Cheese

- **Comté** — hard aged, virtually zero lactose. France's finest.
- **Gruyère** — hard, aged, safe.
- **Brie** — surprisingly low lactose despite being soft.
- **Camembert** — same as Brie, safe in normal portions.
- **Chèvre (goat cheese)** — naturally lower lactose than cow cheese.
- **Emmental / Parmesan** — hard, aged, zero issues.

> France has the world's best cheese. Aged/hard varieties are all safe. This is where you indulge guilt-free.

### Salads & vegetables

- **Salade Niçoise végétarienne** — ask for potatoes, green beans, tomatoes, olives only. Skip tuna and egg. Say "sans thon, sans oeuf, sans oignon."
- **Ratatouille** — cooked zucchini, capsicum, eggplant, tomato. Perfect for you.
- **Grilled vegetables (légumes grillés)** — common side dish, all cooked.
- **Haricots verts (French green beans)** — low FODMAP, classic side.
- **Salade verte (green salad)** — simple lettuce with vinaigrette.
- **Carottes râpées** — grated carrot salad, common starter.

### Mains & proteins

- **Risotto** — rice based, common in Nice (Italian influence). Perfect safe base.
- **Plain pasta** with olive oil / pesto / light tomato sauce.
- **Gratin de légumes** — baked vegetables with cheese topping. Comforting Provençal classic.
- **Pommes frites (fries)** — as a side with any main. Safe and satisfying.
- **Soupe au pistou (Provençal pesto soup)** — vegetable soup with basil. Ask "sans haricots blancs" (no white beans).

> You're vegetarian, no eggs. Focus on risotto, pasta, pizza, galettes, cheese-based dishes, and grilled vegetables. Nice's Italian influence is your biggest advantage — great veggie options everywhere. Always ask "sans oeuf" when ordering.

### Breakfast

- **Fresh baguette with butter and jam** — your go-to French breakfast. No eggs.
- **Galette (buckwheat crêpe)** — egg-free batter. With cheese, tomato, or Nutella.
- **Fresh fruit plate** — berries, banana, grapes, kiwi. Available at most hotels. Avoid watermelon (high FODMAP).
- **Yoghurt (plain)** — French yoghurt is naturally fermented, low lactose.
- **Focaccia or fougasse with olive oil** — egg-free bread options.

> Skip croissants, pain au chocolat, omelettes, and all pastries at hotel buffets — all contain eggs. Your breakfast: baguette + butter + jam + yoghurt + banana + coffee. Simple and satisfying.

### Desserts & sweets

- **Sorbet (fruit flavours)** — dairy free, egg free, your safest dessert. Lemon sorbet is classic Provence.
- **Dark chocolate** — low FODMAP, anti-inflammatory. Safe treat.
- **Fresh fruit with honey** — simple, safe, available everywhere.
- **Chocolate truffles** — small portions, check for egg-free. Many are just cream + chocolate.

### Drinks

- **Espresso / café allongé** — 1 cup, morning only (your rule).
- **Still water (eau plate)** — always order this, stay hydrated.
- **Herbal tea (tisane)** — peppermint (menthe), chamomile (camomille), verbena (verveine) widely available.
- **Fresh lemon juice (citron pressé)** — refreshing, gut-safe.
- **Ginger tea** — ask for "tisane de gingembre" at cafés.

### Provençal specialties

- **Tapenade (olive paste on bread)** — safe, delicious.
- **Pan bagnat végétarien** — Nice sandwich with tomato, capsicum, olives, olive oil in bread. Ask "sans thon, sans oeuf, sans oignon."

> Pissaladière (onion tart) — iconic Provence but onion-heavy and may contain eggs. Skip, or one small taste only. Socca (chickpea flatbread) — small portion only, see the moderate section below.

### Nice's Italian influence

- **Margherita pizza** — thin crust, tomato, mozzarella. Safe.
- **Penne / spaghetti** with pesto or light tomato sauce.
- **Gnocchi** — potato based, gentle. Ask "sans oeuf" — some recipes include eggs in the dough, many don't.
- **Bruschetta (without raw garlic)** — toasted bread with tomato.
- **Caprese salad** — mozzarella, tomato, basil. Perfect.

> Nice's Italian heritage means great pizza, pasta, and risotto options everywhere. This is your safest cuisine zone.

## Eat moderately — small portions

### Dairy & cream

- **Cream-based sauces (béchamel, cream pasta)** — French cooking uses heavy cream liberally. Small portions only. Fat + cream triggers bile release.
- **Tarte aux légumes (vegetable tart)** — pastry + cream + cheese. Confirm it's egg-free or skip. Small portion only.
- **Gratin dauphinois (potato gratin)** — cream-heavy, some recipes include eggs. Ask "sans oeuf" or skip. Small side portion only.
- **Croque fromage (cheese-only version)** — no ham, but the béchamel may contain eggs. Ask "sans oeuf" or skip. Heavy — share rather than eating a full one.

### Breads & grains

- **Pain complet (whole wheat bread)** — higher fibre than white baguette. 1–2 slices max.
- **Sourdough** — better than regular wheat but still a moderate trigger for you. One serving per day max.
- **Socca (chickpea flatbread)** — Nice specialty, made from chickpea flour. A small portion (2–3 pieces) should be okay. Don't eat a full plate — chickpea = high FODMAP in large amounts.
- **Galette (buckwheat crêpe)** — buckwheat is gentle, but fillings often include cream, mushrooms, or heavy cheese.

### Fruits & desserts

- **Stone fruits (peaches, cherries, apricots)** — Provence is famous for these. Moderate FODMAP. 1–2 pieces okay, don't gorge at a market.
- **Mango sorbet** — fructose. Small scoop only.
- **Ice cream (glace)** — lactose varies by brand. Sorbet is always safer.
- **Dried fruits at markets** — dates, figs, dried apricots are high FODMAP. A few pieces only.

### Drinks

- **Wine** — a small glass with dinner is generally tolerated. Red wine has histamine (could trigger head pressure) and raises uric acid. White/rosé is gentler. Provence rosé is world-class — one small glass at dinner is reasonable. No more than one glass/day given your uric acid sensitivity.
- **Orange juice** — moderate fructose. Small glass at breakfast, don't chug a large one.
- **Second coffee** — you're on vacation, one extra may be tempting. Resist. Your gut rule still applies.

### Specific dishes

- **French onion soup (soupe à l'oignon)** — iconic but onion-heavy. A few spoonfuls to taste, don't eat a full bowl.
- **Soupe de légumes (vegetable soup)** — usually safe but check for an onion/leek base. A light bowl is okay.
- **Tian Provençal (layered vegetable bake)** — zucchini, tomato, eggplant. Often has some cream/cheese. Small portion.
- **Couscous** — moderate fibre. Small portion as a side, not the main dish.

### Châteauneuf wine tour (Day 2)

- **Wine tasting** — sip and spit where possible. Multiple tastings = cumulative histamine and alcohol load.
- **Cheese platters at wineries** — aged cheeses are safe, but watch cream/fresh varieties.
- **Eat bread between tastings** to buffer your stomach.
- **Hydrate:** 1 glass of water for every wine tasted.

> Alcohol on an empty stomach + fibro = guaranteed flare risk. Always eat before and during tastings.

## Avoid — known triggers

### High-risk French dishes

- **Cassoulet** — white bean (haricots blancs) stew. Same category as rajma. Guaranteed trigger.
- **Lentil dishes (salade de lentilles)** — French green lentils are high FODMAP. Common in Provence. Skip.
- **Choucroute (sauerkraut)** — fermented cabbage, gas-producing, histamine-rich.
- **Fondue / Raclette** — massive amounts of melted cheese + cream = bile overload.
- **Escargot** — non-vegetarian (snails) and drenched in garlic butter. Skip on both counts.

### Contains eggs / non-veg

- **Croissants** — contain eggs in the dough and glaze. Skip.
- **Pain au chocolat** — same as croissant. Skip.
- **Most French pastries** — almost all contain eggs. Stick to baguette and focaccia.
- **Quiche (all varieties)** — egg + cream base. Skip.
- **Omelette** — eggs. Skip.
- **Regular crêpes** — the batter contains eggs. Choose galettes (buckwheat) instead.
- **Soufflé** — egg-based. Skip.
- **Mousse au chocolat** — egg-based. Skip. Choose sorbet or dark chocolate instead.
- **Macarons** — contain egg whites. Skip.
- **Clafoutis** — egg-based baked custard. Skip.
- **Crème brûlée** — egg-based custard. Skip.
- **Panna cotta** — may contain eggs. Verify or skip.
- **Tarte aux fruits** — the pastry cream filling contains eggs. Skip.
- **Tarte aux pommes (apple tart)** — apple is high FODMAP and the pastry contains eggs. Double skip.
- **Salade Niçoise (standard)** — contains tuna + egg. Order the végétarienne version only.
- **Croque monsieur (standard)** — contains ham. Order "croque fromage" only.
- **Any charcuterie** — all non-vegetarian.

### Ingredients to watch

- **Raw onion (oignon cru)** — in salads, sandwiches. Ask "sans oignon" everywhere.
- **Heavy garlic (ail)** — French cooking uses lots. Light garlic in cooked dishes is usually okay. Garlic-heavy aioli, garlic bread, or garlic soup — skip.
- **Mushrooms (champignons)** — very common in French cuisine. High FODMAP (mannitol). Avoid mushroom risotto, mushroom pizza, mushroom sauces.
- **Artichokes** — common in Provence markets. Very high FODMAP. Skip entirely.
- **Leeks (poireaux)** — in soups and tarts. High FODMAP fructans, same family as onion.
- **Beetroot (betterave)** — common in French salads. Can trigger urgency based on your experience. Skip or take a very small amount.
- **Asparagus (asperges)** — high FODMAP and high purine (uric acid risk). Common in Provence spring menus. Skip.

### Drinks & others

- **Carbonated drinks / sparkling water (eau gazeuse)** — gas. Order eau plate (still).
- **Beer** — carbonation + gluten + histamine. Triple trigger.
- **Pastis / absinthe** — anise-based spirits common in Provence. Strong gut irritant.
- **Heavy cocktails** — sugar + alcohol + mixers = gut bomb.
- **Apple cider (cidre)** — apple + fermentation + carbonation.
- **Multiple coffees per day** — vacation mode doesn't change your gut rule.

## Practical tips for France

### Key French phrases

| French | English |
|---|---|
| "Je suis végétarien strict — pas de viande, pas de poisson, pas d'oeufs" | I'm a strict vegetarian — no meat, no fish, no eggs |
| "Sans oignon, s'il vous plaît" | Without onion, please |
| "Sans oeuf" | Without egg |
| "Sans ail" | Without garlic |
| "Sans champignons" | Without mushrooms |
| "Pas trop de crème" | Not too much cream |
| "Eau plate" | Still water |
| "Tisane de menthe / camomille" | Peppermint / chamomile tea |
| "Allergie aux oignons" | Onion allergy — restaurants take allergies more seriously than preferences |
| "C'est végétarien? Il y a des oeufs?" | Is this vegetarian? Are there eggs? |

### Hotel breakfast strategy

French hotel breakfasts typically have: croissants (contain eggs — skip), baguette, butter, jam, yoghurt, fruit, juice, coffee. Take your Econorm 30 minutes before. Have your coffee + baguette with butter & jam + yoghurt + banana. Skip croissants, pain au chocolat, omelettes, and all pastries. Skip muesli/granola (often contains dried fruits and bran). Skip large OJ.

- **Auberge de Cassagne** — your "Gastronomy" package includes breakfast. Ask what's egg-free — baguette, cheese, yoghurt, and fruit will always be available.
- **Le Méridien Nice** — hotel breakfast buffet, same approach.

### Monaco GP day (June 7)

A long day in the grandstand. Pack safe snacks: banana, almonds, a plain baguette sandwich (cheese + tomato, no onion). Stay hydrated — carry a water bottle. The heat + crowd + excitement + fibro = high flare risk. Take peppermint oil before any heavy meal. Don't skip meals — blood sugar drops trigger both gut urgency and head pressure.

Post-race taxi back to Nice — eat light that evening. Your gut will be stressed from an unusual day.

### Travel supplement kit

- **Must pack:** Econorm strips (remaining course), NOW peppermint oil gels, magnesium glycinate (both doses), L-theanine, chamomile teabags (French cafés have tisane, but bring backup).
- **Keep in carry-on:** Peppermint oil + Econorm + magnesium. Checked luggage can get lost — supplements can't be easily replaced in France.
- **Timezone:** India → France = −3.5 hours. Adjust supplement timing to local meals, not India time. Your body adapts faster than your phone.

### Markets & street food (Provence)

Provençal markets (Avignon, Saint-Rémy, Les Baux) will tempt you with: olives ✓, cheese ✓, bread ✓, tapenade ✓, lavender honey ✓, dried fruits (small amounts only), socca (small portion only).

- **Avoid at markets:** large quantities of dried figs/dates, bean salads, strong garlic preparations. Most charcuterie/sausages are non-veg — skip entirely.
- Graze small amounts across the day rather than one large market meal. Multiple small safe portions beat one large risky one.

### Fibro + travel management

- **Jet lag:** Minimal (3.5 hr shift). Adjust meal/supplement times on day 1.
- **Walking:** Nice, Avignon, and the markets all involve walking. Pace yourself — 15 min walk, then rest. Carry water always.
- **Heat:** Late May/June in Provence = 25–30°C. Heat worsens fibro fatigue. Stay in shade, wear a hat, drink 3L+ water daily.
- **Rest day (June 3):** Use it. The Auberge spa is your recovery tool. Don't feel guilty about resting while on vacation.
- **Flare protocol while abroad:** Chamomile tea + magnesium + C-curve stretch + rest in a cool room. Same as home.

## Safe daily eating template

### Breakfast

- Econorm 30 minutes before.
- Baguette with butter & jam (no croissants — eggs).
- Plain yoghurt with banana.
- 1 coffee (café allongé or espresso).
- Magnesium + L-theanine with breakfast.

### Lunch (main meal)

- Peppermint oil 30 minutes before.
- Risotto / pasta / pizza margherita / galette.
- Side salad (no raw onion).
- Cheese plate (aged varieties).
- Still water throughout.

### Dinner (lighter)

- Econorm + peppermint oil 30 minutes before.
- Ratatouille / grilled vegetables / light pasta.
- Or a simple galette (cheese + vegetables).
- Small dessert: sorbet or dark chocolate.
- Magnesium at 7 PM with dinner.
- Chamomile tea before bed.

## The golden rules

**Don't stack triggers.** One indulgence per meal, one borderline food per day. If you had cream sauce at lunch, go plain at dinner. If you tried socca as a snack, keep dinner rice/pasta based. Your gut's daily budget hasn't changed — you're just spending it on French food instead of Indian food.

**Emergency phrase:** "Où sont les toilettes?" (Where are the toilets?) — Learn this one. French restaurants and cafés always have a toilet. Monaco GP grandstands have facilities nearby.

**Remember:** You're strictly vegetarian — no meat, no fish, no eggs. Many French "vegetarian" options contain hidden eggs (quiche, omelette, regular crêpes, mousse, soufflé). Always ask "sans oeuf" and "je suis végétarien strict — pas de viande, pas de poisson, pas d'oeufs." Galettes (buckwheat crêpes) are your safest crêpe option, as the batter is naturally egg-free. For desserts, sorbet is always safe — mousse and soufflé contain eggs.
